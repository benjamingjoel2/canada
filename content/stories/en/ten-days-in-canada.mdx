We get this question more than any other: "We have ten days. What can we see?"

The honest answer is: a lot, as long as it's not everything. Ten days is a good amount of time for one region of Canada, or two that sit next to each other. It isn't enough to cross the country, and the people who try usually come home tired.

## The maths

Let's be practical. Ten days isn't really ten days.

- **Day one** is mostly a long-haul flight. From London or Paris, that's about seven hours to Toronto or Montréal, and around nine and a half to ten to Vancouver. You'll land in the afternoon, a few time zones behind, and want an early night.
- **The last day** is a departure. Most flights to Europe leave in the evening, so you get a morning, but not much more.
- **Every internal flight** costs you about half a day, once you add getting to the airport, check-in and the drive at the other end.

So ten days on paper is usually eight days of actually being somewhere. Each extra region you add takes away another half day, or a whole one.

## What doesn't work

Here's a version we see often:

Toronto, Niagara Falls, Montréal, Québec City, Banff, Lake Louise, Jasper and Vancouver. In ten days.

It's possible, technically. It's also roughly four flights, two long drives and a lot of hotel check-ins. You'd spend about a third of your holiday in transit. You'd see the famous views, briefly, from a car park.

We'd much rather plan you one of these.

## Three trips that work

### The Rockies and the coast

- **Days 1–2:** Vancouver. Stanley Park, Granville Island, a good dinner. Recover from the flight.
- **Day 3:** Fly to Calgary (about an hour and a half), then drive to Banff, about another ninety minutes.
- **Days 4–5:** Banff and Lake Louise, with early starts to beat the crowds.
- **Day 6:** The Icefields Parkway, all day, slowly.
- **Days 7–8:** Jasper. Maligne Lake, wildlife, a dark sky.
- **Day 9:** Back towards Calgary or Edmonton.
- **Day 10:** Fly home.

You'll miss Vancouver Island. That's fine. It'll still be there.

### Québec, slowly

- **Days 1–3:** Montréal. Old Montréal, the markets, Mount Royal, bagels.
- **Day 4:** Train or drive to Québec City, about three hours.
- **Days 5–6:** Québec City. The old town, the walls, the river.
- **Days 7–8:** Charlevoix, about an hour and a half east. Small inns, good food, the river widening towards the sea.
- **Day 9:** Tadoussac, for whales in season (roughly May to October). Then back towards Québec City.
- **Day 10:** Fly home from Québec City or Montréal.

One region, almost no flying, and very good lunches.

### Winter in the Yukon, with a city

- **Days 1–2:** Vancouver, to break the journey.
- **Day 3:** Fly to Whitehorse, about two and a half hours.
- **Days 4–7:** A cabin outside Whitehorse. Aurora at night (we plan for three or four chances, because clouds don't take requests). Dog sledding, snowshoeing, hot springs by day.
- **Day 8:** Fly back to Vancouver.
- **Days 9–10:** A quiet couple of days, then home.

## A few rules of thumb

- **Count nights, not places.** Fewer than two nights somewhere means you mostly see the hotel.
- **One flight is fine. Two is a lot. Three is a different trip.**
- **Leave a spare day** for weather, especially in the mountains or the North.
- **Don't save the best for the last day.** If a flight is delayed, that's the day you lose.

## If you have more time

Two weeks lets you add a second region, like Vancouver Island or the Atlantic coast. Three weeks lets you think about crossing the country, ideally with a few nights on a train.

But if you have ten days, that's still a very good trip. Pick the part of Canada you most want to see, and give it the time it deserves. You'll see more by trying to see less.
