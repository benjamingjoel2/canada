Polar bear season is about six weeks long. We'll tell you which six. First, though, a little about why it happens, because once you understand the bears, the planning makes sense.

## Why Churchill

Churchill is a town of fewer than a thousand people on the western shore of Hudson Bay, in northern Manitoba. It's best known for one thing, and that thing weighs up to 600 kilograms.

Polar bears hunt seals from the sea ice. In summer, the ice on Hudson Bay melts and the bears come ashore, where they mostly rest and wait, eating very little. In autumn they gather along the coast near Churchill, because the bay tends to freeze there first. The moment the ice is thick enough, they walk out onto it and go back to hunting.

So for a few weeks each year, several hundred hungry bears are waiting on a stretch of coast with a small town on it, which happens to have an airport and a railway. That's the whole secret.

## When to go

The bears gather from around mid-October. Most years the peak is from late October to mid-November. Once the bay freezes, usually in the second half of November, they leave within days.

That means:

- **Late October to mid-November** is the core of the season, and the most reliable.
- **Early October** is quieter and slightly cheaper, with fewer bears.
- **Late November** is a gamble. If the ice comes early, the bears go early.

The sea ice is forming later than it used to, and the dates have shifted over the past few decades. We check with people on the ground every year before we suggest a week.

## How you actually see them

You don't walk out and look for them. Churchill takes bear safety seriously, and so should you.

**Tundra vehicles.** Most visitors see bears from large, high-sided vehicles that drive along set trails in the conservation area east of town. You sit well above the ground, with windows and an open-air deck at the back. Bears sometimes walk right up to the vehicle and look in. It's warm inside, which matters more than you'd think.

**Tundra lodges.** A few operators run lodges on the tundra itself, built like a train of linked units on wheels. You sleep where the bears are, and see them at dawn and dusk when the day-trippers have gone. Rooms are small and cosy, and they book up a long way ahead.

**Fly-in coastal lodges.** A small number of remote lodges along the coast offer guided walks at ground level with very experienced, armed guides. It's a different, more intense experience. Space is very limited and it isn't for everyone.

You'll also likely see Arctic foxes, ptarmigan and, if you're lucky, the northern lights. In November, the aurora over Churchill is a bonus, not a promise.

## Getting there

There are no roads to Churchill. You have two options:

- **Fly from Winnipeg.** Around two hours.
- **Take the train from Winnipeg.** It takes around two days and covers about 1,700 kilometres. It shows you how remote Churchill is. It also runs late. We'd only suggest it one way, with time to spare.

Most trips spend a night in Winnipeg on each side, because Prairie weather doesn't always respect flight schedules.

## What it costs, honestly

Polar bear trips are expensive. Everything in Churchill has to be flown or railed in, the season is short, and the vehicles and lodges are specialist kit with limited space. A short trip of four or five nights costs more than most people expect, and the tundra lodges cost more again.

We'll give you a clear figure early on, so you can decide whether it's for you before you've fallen in love with it.

## What to bring

It's cold. Expect minus 10 to minus 20 °C, with a wind off the bay that makes it feel colder. You'll want:

- A proper insulated parka, or hire one in Churchill (we can arrange it).
- Insulated boots rated well below freezing.
- Thin gloves for the camera and thick mitts over them.
- A neck gaiter and a hat that covers your ears.
- A camera with a decent zoom. Phones do well up close, but you'll want more reach.

## A few honest notes

- **We can't guarantee bears.** Nobody can. But in the right two or three weeks, most guests see several, often many.
- **Book early.** For the best weeks, a year ahead is normal. Eighteen months isn't strange.
- **There's a summer option.** In July and August, a few lodges on the coast offer beluga whales, wildflowers and the chance of a polar bear on the shore. Fewer bears, much warmer, and very good in its own way.
- **Churchill is a real town.** People live and work there. There's a holding facility for bears that wander too close, known locally as the polar bear jail. Bears that end up there are later released away from town, sometimes by helicopter, sometimes once the bay has frozen. Visitors should follow every rule they're given.
