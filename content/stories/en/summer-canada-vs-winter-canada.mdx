Canada is two countries for the price of one: summer Canada and winter Canada. They share a map and not much else. The same lake is a turquoise postcard in July and a flat white field you can walk across in February. The same town is busy with hikers in August and quiet enough to hear the snow in January.

Neither is better. They're just different trips, and it helps to know which one you're booking.

## Summer Canada (roughly late June to mid-September)

This is the Canada most people picture. Long days, open trails, boats on the water.

**What's good:**

- **Daylight.** In the Yukon in June you get around 19 hours of it in Whitehorse, and in Dawson City it barely gets dark at all. Even in the south, it's light until after 9 p.m.
- **The mountains are open.** Snow leaves the high trails in the Rockies around early July. Alpine meadows flower from mid-July. Lake Louise is properly thawed by June, and the road to Moraine Lake is open, though only by shuttle or tour; private cars haven't been allowed since 2023.
- **Wildlife on the water.** Whales off Vancouver Island, in Tadoussac and in Newfoundland. Belugas in Churchill. Puffins on the Atlantic coast.
- **Easy logistics.** Ferries run full timetables, floatplanes fly most days, and everything that's seasonal is actually open.

**What's less good:**

- **Other people.** The Rockies in July and August are busy, and the best lodges book up a year ahead.
- **Prices.** Summer is high season almost everywhere.
- **Bugs.** In the North and in canoe country, June and early July are mosquito and blackfly season. Bring a head net. This is not a joke.
- **Wildfire smoke.** Some summers, smoke from fires in the West can cloud the views for days. We watch the forecasts and move plans where we can.

## Winter Canada (roughly December to March)

Winter here is cold, bright and much more enjoyable than people expect, provided you dress for it.

**What's good:**

- **The northern lights.** From late August to April, the Yukon and Yellowknife have dark skies under the auroral oval. Winter adds snow on the ground and frozen lakes to stand on while you watch.
- **Snow that behaves.** The Rockies and BC get dry, deep snow from January to March. Skiing, snowshoeing, skating on lakes, dog sledding.
- **Québec in winter.** Old Québec under snow is very good, and the Québec Winter Carnival runs for a couple of weeks from late January into February.
- **Quiet.** Lake Louise in February has fewer people and a skating rink where the canoes used to be.

**What's less good:**

- **Cold.** In Whitehorse or Yellowknife, minus 20 to minus 30 °C is normal on a January night, and minus 40 happens. We arrange proper cold-weather gear.
- **Short days.** Whitehorse gets under six hours of daylight at the December solstice. Plan the long drives for the morning.
- **Things close.** Many lodges, boat tours and mountain roads shut for the season. Some places you'd visit in summer simply aren't an option.

## The in-between months

**September** is our favourite month, quietly. The crowds have gone, the larches in the Rockies turn gold, the grizzlies are on the salmon rivers, and the first aurora appears in the North. Weather can turn fast, so we leave some room.

**Late September to mid-October** is fall colour season in Québec and Ontario.

**October and November** bring the polar bears to Churchill. It's not winter yet on the calendar, but it will feel like it.

**April, May and early November** are awkward. Snow is melting or arriving, trails are muddy, and many places are closed between seasons. We still plan trips then, but we're honest about what you'll miss.

## How to choose

Some simple questions help.

**What do you most want to see?** Bears on salmon rivers: September. Polar bears: October or November. Aurora: winter, or early autumn if you'd rather not freeze. Hiking in the Rockies: July to September. Whales: summer.

**How do you feel about cold?** If the idea of minus 25 °C makes you unhappy, a winter trip will too. If it sounds like an adventure, you'll probably love it.

**Do you mind other people?** Summer is busier. The quiet months need more flexibility.

**When can you actually travel?** School holidays narrow things down quickly. A family trip in July is a good trip. It just needs booking early.

## Or both

Some of our guests come twice. Once in summer, for the mountains and the coast. Once in winter, for the aurora and the quiet. They tell us it felt like visiting two different countries, which is more or less the point.
