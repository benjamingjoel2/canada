Banff deserves its reputation. It's also the busiest place in the Canadian Rockies by a long way, and in July you'll share the famous viewpoints with a lot of other people holding phones.

The good news is that Banff is just one national park in a chain of them. The Canadian Rockies run for well over 1,000 kilometres, and some of the best parts are a short drive from where most people stop.

## Jasper

Jasper is Banff's quieter, wilder neighbour to the north, and the largest of the Rocky Mountain national parks at about 11,000 square kilometres.

The town is smaller and less polished. The wildlife is easier to see: elk on the edge of town, bighorn sheep on the roadside, and in the right season, black bears and the occasional grizzly. Maligne Lake, about an hour from town, is long, cold and very blue, and the boat trip to Spirit Island is worth the early start.

Jasper is also a Dark Sky Preserve, which means real stars on a clear night and, in autumn and winter, a decent chance of the northern lights.

In July 2024 a wildfire burned through part of the park and destroyed around a third of the buildings in town. Jasper is open and rebuilding, and visitors are welcome. Some trails and areas are still recovering, and we check what's open before we plan your days.

## The Icefields Parkway

Between Lake Louise and Jasper runs the Icefields Parkway: 232 kilometres of road past glaciers, waterfalls and turquoise lakes. You can drive it in about three hours. Please don't.

Give it a full day, or better, break it with a night in the middle. Peyto Lake, the Columbia Icefield and Athabasca Falls are the well-known stops, but the quieter pullouts are often just as good. There's no mobile signal for most of the drive and only one place to buy fuel in summer, so fill up before you go.

The Athabasca Glacier, next to the road at the Columbia Icefield, has retreated by more than 1.5 kilometres in the last 125 years or so. Small signs along the path show where the ice was in different years. It's a quiet, slightly unsettling walk.

## Yoho

Yoho is just over the border into British Columbia, about an hour west of Banff. It's small, steep and full of water.

- **Emerald Lake** is the colour its name suggests, with a flat trail all the way around it.
- **Takakkaw Falls** is one of the highest waterfalls in Canada, and you can walk right up to the bottom of it.
- **Lake O'Hara** is the one people talk about quietly. Access is by a limited bus, booked through a Parks Canada reservation system, and places go fast. If you get a seat, it's one of the best hiking areas in the Rockies.

Yoho also holds the Burgess Shale, a fossil bed more than 500 million years old. You can only visit with a guided hike, which is worth doing if you like that kind of thing.

## Kootenay

Kootenay National Park runs south from near Banff along the road towards Radium Hot Springs. It's quieter, narrower and greener. Marble Canyon is a short walk over a deep, narrow gorge, and the Paint Pots are ochre-coloured springs that Indigenous peoples have used as a source of pigment for a very long time.

Radium Hot Springs, at the south end, is a good place to finish a day of driving.

## Kananaskis

Kananaskis Country sits just east of Banff and about an hour from Calgary, outside the national parks. It's where many people from Calgary go to hike, and you'll see why: big mountains, fewer coaches, good trails. You'll need a Kananaskis Conservation Pass for your car, which we'll arrange.

## Waterton Lakes

Waterton is in the far south-west corner of Alberta, about three hours from Calgary, where the prairie runs straight into the mountains with almost no foothills in between. It's small, windy and very beautiful, and it connects across the border with Glacier National Park in Montana.

Parts of the park are still recovering from the 2017 Kenow wildfire. The wildflowers in early summer have come back strongly.

## How to fit it together

A realistic Rockies trip of ten to twelve days might look like this:

- **Calgary to Kananaskis or Canmore** for two nights, to settle in.
- **Banff and Lake Louise** for three nights, with an early start for the famous lakes. Moraine Lake is reached by shuttle or tour; private cars aren't allowed.
- **A day trip into Yoho**, or a night at Emerald Lake.
- **The Icefields Parkway** with a night at the halfway point.
- **Jasper** for three nights, then out by train towards Vancouver, or back to Edmonton or Calgary to fly.

Waterton and Kootenay fit best into a longer trip, or a second visit.

## The short version

Banff is the doorway, not the house. Stay a couple of nights, then keep going. The mountains don't stop at the town limits, and most of the people do.
