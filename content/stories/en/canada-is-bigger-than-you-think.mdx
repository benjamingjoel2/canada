Most people who call us have a map of Canada in their heads. Most of those maps are wrong, and not in the direction you'd hope.

Canada covers almost 10 million square kilometres. It's the second-largest country on Earth, after Russia. It has six time zones, one of which is offset by half an hour, just to keep you honest. On a flat map it looks like a wide country with a few big cities along the bottom. In practice it's several very different countries joined by long flights.

None of this is a reason not to come. It's a reason to plan with real numbers.

## Some distances, without the adjectives

Vancouver to Halifax is about 4,400 kilometres in a straight line. London to Cairo is about 3,500. Plan accordingly.

A few more, in the units that matter:

- **Toronto to Vancouver:** around four and a half to five hours flying west, a little less coming back. That's longer than London to Istanbul.
- **Toronto to Vancouver by road:** roughly 4,400 kilometres. If you drive it without detours and without sleeping much, you'll need four or five long days.
- **Toronto to Vancouver by train:** four nights. Some people love this. We'll come back to it.
- **Montréal to Québec City:** about 250 kilometres, or three hours by road or rail. Close, by Canadian standards.
- **Calgary to Banff:** about an hour and a half by road.
- **Banff to Jasper:** 288 kilometres along the Icefields Parkway. About three and a half hours if you don't stop. You will stop.
- **Vancouver to Tofino:** a ferry of about an hour and a half, then three hours of winding road. Five to six hours door to door, or under an hour by small plane.
- **Winnipeg to Churchill:** there is no road. You fly, about two hours, or take the train, which takes around two days.

## Why it catches people out

Three reasons, mostly.

**Maps flatten the north.** Most world maps stretch everything near the poles, so the north looks huge and empty, while the gaps between southern cities look smaller than they are.

**Roads don't go straight.** Mountains, lakes and fjords mean the road between two points can be twice the straight-line distance. The British Columbia coast is a good example: a lodge 150 kilometres from Vancouver may have no road at all.

**Flights connect.** Getting from Whitehorse to Halifax, say, usually means going through Vancouver and then Toronto or Montréal. A trip that looks like one flight is often three, and most of a day.

## What this means for your trip

The most useful thing we can tell you is this: one or two regions per trip, not five.

On a two-week journey, every long transfer costs you most of a day. Fly from Vancouver to Toronto in the morning, and by the time you've crossed three time zones and checked in, the day has gone. Do that three times and you've lost three days of a fourteen-day holiday to airports.

Here's what works, roughly:

- **Ten days:** one region, or two neighbouring ones. The Rockies and Vancouver. Montréal, Québec City and Charlevoix. The Yukon in winter.
- **Two weeks:** two regions with one flight between them. The West Coast and the Rockies. Québec and the Atlantic.
- **Three weeks or more:** now you can think about crossing the country, ideally with a few nights on a train so the crossing becomes part of the trip.

## The train question

Every so often someone asks whether they can see Canada by train. You can see a lot of it. The long-distance train from Toronto to Vancouver crosses the lakes and forests of northern Ontario, the prairies and the Rockies over four nights. It's comfortable and slow on purpose. It is not a fast way to get anywhere, and it runs late often enough that we never plan a tight connection at the other end.

If that sounds appealing, it's one of the best ways to understand the size of the place. If it sounds like four nights of trees, it probably is, for you.

## Things that are closer than they look

To be fair to Canada, a few things are easier than people expect.

Niagara Falls is about an hour and a half from Toronto. Whistler is two hours from Vancouver on a very good road. Old Québec is a short walk from your hotel, if we've chosen your hotel well. And from Western Europe, Montréal and Toronto are only about seven or eight hours away, which is less than many people fly to get somewhere warm.

## The short version

Canada is big. Not "big country" big, but "plan your trip in hours, not kilometres" big. Choose fewer places, stay longer in each, and leave some space for the weather.
